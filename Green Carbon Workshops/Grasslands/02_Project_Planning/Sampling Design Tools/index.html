What this does and does not show

The surface is simulated, so the “true mean” is knowable and the estimate can be checked against it. In the field it never is — the interval is all you have. Samples are placed at random, the estimate is a simple mean, and the interval uses Student's t on n−1 degrees of freedom. Roots are drawn with more than twice the coefficient of variation of soil carbon, which is why the same number of samples buys a much wider interval. That is the whole argument for setting the two targets separately. Nothing here replaces the Sample Allocation Calculator, which sizes a real campaign from your own prior.
